Learning where the drone flies next

A UAV collects sensor data from cluster heads that only wake up when it promises to arrive. In Phase 3 a PPO agent learned the UAV's next stop, inside the ns-3 simulation. What Phase 3 built

  1. A bridge between ns-3 and Python. The simulator pauses at every decision, sends what the UAV knows over a pipe, and waits for the next cluster. A new external routing policy in C++.
  2. The learning problem. 13 features per cluster, all from the UAV's own records: last reported buffer and energy, promise slack, missed meetings, lateness estimate. No future information, checked by a test.
  3. The reward. Data delivered, minus UAV energy, broken promises, cluster-head listening energy, and (for ER-PPO) the risk of arriving late.
  4. The agent. MaskablePPO with a per-cluster scoring network and self-attention, warm-started by copying the tour, then trained for 300 000 decisions (about 20 minutes per variant).
  5. The ablation. A1 plain PPO, A2 adds the lateness estimate, A3 adds adaptive wake windows, A4 adds the risk term (full ER-PPO).

Why PPO matches the tour instead of beating it

Tested on 5 held-out hotspot networks with small buffers, where the tour loses 8.9 % of data to overflow. If any policy could beat the tour, it would be here.

PPO chose the tour's next stop at 100 % of decisions. Hand-written detours fly 279 m per visit against the tour's 129 m, and every skipped cluster misses the window it was promised. Under promise-based wake-up, changing the visiting order costs more than it gains, so the tour is close to optimal here.

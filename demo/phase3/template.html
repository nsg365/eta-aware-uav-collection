Promise-based wake scheduling for UAV data collection

A UAV collects data from cluster heads (CHs) of a sensor network with no base station. Two decisions matter: which cluster the UAV visits next, and when each CH turns its Wi-Fi on. We link them with one rule (the UAV promises each CH when it will be back, and the CH sleeps until then), build the whole system in ns-3, and compare heuristics, the shortest tour, PPO and a published Q-learning method.

PPO vs shortest tour: no significant difference (PDR 0.973 vs 0.972) vs best heuristic: +6.6 pts PDR, 3.6× meetings on time vs Q-learning baseline: +25 pts PDR scales from 100 to 2000 sensors without retraining decision time: 1.5 ms

How the system works

  1. Clustering and CH election (offline). Sensors are grouped with DBSCAN (eps = 0.8 × 80 m sensor range). A sensor may be CH only if it reaches every member and is within 30 m of the cluster's meeting point; clusters with fewer than two such sensors are split. Each candidate i gets the score S_i = 0.5\,\frac{E_i}{E_0} + 0.5\left(1 - \frac{d_i}{R_\mathrm{UAV}}\right) where E_i is its remaining energy, E_0 the starting energy, d_i its distance to the meeting point and R_\mathrm{UAV} = 30 m. The best is primary CH, the second is backup.
  2. ns-3 simulator (C++). The CH–UAV link is real ns-3 802.11g Wi-Fi (6 Mb/s, 16 dBm) with ns-3's radio energy model at ESP32 currents. The 30 m UAV range comes from a link sweep: the largest distance with ≥ 99 % delivery under fading. Sensor-to-CH hops use CC2420 energy per reading. The UAV flies at 30 m with per-leg speed noise; flight 150 W, hover 170 W. The primary CH copies its buffer to the backup every second and sends heartbeats; three missed heartbeats and the backup takes over.
  3. Promise-then-honour wake windows. At the end of every visit to cluster k at time t, the UAV promises to return at T_k = t + P, \qquad \text{CH listens only in } \left[\,T_k - w_e,\; T_k + w_l\,\right] where P is the time of one tour lap and w_e = 20 s. Fixed window: w_l = 20 s. Adaptive window: w_l = q_{0.95}, the 95th percentile of how late the UAV has been at past visits. A missed window falls back to up to five short retries, then low-power discovery. The mission starts with one survey lap that makes the first promises.
  4. What the UAV knows. It never sees true buffers. For each cluster it keeps a belief: data rate (moving average of what it collected), estimated buffer, CH energy at the last visit, time since contact, promise slack, missed meetings and its lateness record. Every policy plans from this belief only.

The PPO agent

  1. ns-3 ↔ Python bridge. A new external routing policy in C++ pauses the simulation at every decision, sends the UAV's belief over a pipe and waits for the chosen cluster.
  2. Observation. 13 features per cluster (flight time, relative position, belief values above, lateness estimate for A2+), plus mission time and UAV position. Clusters are sorted nearest first and padded to 40 slots; invalid choices are masked. A test checks that no future or true-state information leaks in.
  3. Reward. After each decision R = R_D - 0.2\,R_\mathrm{UAV} - R_\mathrm{miss} - 0.25\,R_\mathrm{CH} - 0.5\,R_\mathrm{risk} R_D is the data collected, R_\mathrm{UAV} the UAV flight and hover energy, R_\mathrm{miss} the promised windows missed, R_\mathrm{CH} the CH listening energy and R_\mathrm{risk} (A4 only) how late the UAV is expected to reach the chosen cluster. A broken promise is penalised as soon as it can no longer be kept.
  4. Network. One small network scores every cluster, with one self-attention layer so each score can depend on the others. The same weights work for any number of clusters.
  5. Training. MaskablePPO on 200 random 100-sensor networks (seeds 101–300), 300 000 decisions (about 20 min on a laptop); evaluation always on unseen seeds 1–10.

Network by network against the matching tour, every PPO variant's difference in PDR, on-time rate and CH energy has a 95 % interval that includes zero. Data greedy keeps a high PDR only because CHs keep listening in retry windows after it breaks a promise, which shows up as 2.5× the CH energy. Nearest feasible keeps its promises but never reaches far clusters. Q-learning's state describes only the nearest cluster, so on unseen networks it often picks at random (its result varies between runs: PDR 0.68–0.72).

Why PPO matches the tour instead of beating it

With promises, every CH sleeps until the time it was told. Any change of order makes the UAV late somewhere else. We tested this in two hard cases.

PPO either learns the tour exactly or leaves it and does worse, like the heuristics. Detours fly 279 m per visit against the tour's 129 m, and every skipped cluster misses its window. Under bursts the UAV only learns of a burst at its next visit, and going back early breaks other promises. So with promise-based wake-up the tour is close to the best order, and PPO learning to match it is the expected result, not a training failure.

Limitations and next steps

  1. Agents were trained on 100-sensor networks with the older noise; retraining on the reduced noise and larger networks (config in the repo) did not finish in time.
  2. To beat the tour a policy needs earlier news of a burst (for example a short status message from a CH) or a way to change promises already made.
  3. The Q-learning baseline is our adaptation with a small state and less training, not a faithful reproduction of the original paper.
  4. UAV battery and return to the depot are not modelled; ablations A7–A9 (no backup CH, takeover on a missed meeting, no survey lap) are implemented, with full results still to run.
